# Overview

The Penny Hockey project was a tabletop game board that combined woodworking, laser cutting, 3D printing, soldering, and Arduino programming. The board features a NASA logo laser engraved into clear plexiglass, surrounded by a wooden frame supported by dado cuts and assembled with miter joints.

# Design Brief

The target was children aged 5 and up. More and more children are drawn to phones and tablets, and those products keep getting more engaging. The aim was a board game that competes for that attention with colour, light and fast gameplay, so children would choose it over a screen.

The constraints:

- completed individually
- standard 9″ × 12″ playing surface
- walls ¾″ wide by 1¼″ tall
- any non-standard materials documented with annotated concept sketches
- within a **$30** budget

## Research

Most penny hockey sets are a flat wooden board with a hole at each end for the penny to drop through. I kept the wooden frame, the "players" that block shots from certain angles, and the rounded inside corners. The rest borrows from air hockey tables: a clear plexiglass surface raised on legs, with a slot through the end walls as the goal. An LCD with pushbuttons keeps score, an LED strip adds light, and a piezo buzzer adds sound.

# CAD Model

The board was modelled in Autodesk Fusion 360. I modelled and dimensioned every part and assembly except the electronics: the LCD ([j.jacck44, 2020](https://www.autodesk.com/community/gallery/project/155964/lcd-1602-i2c)), Arduino Nano ([big_red_frog, 2014](https://www.autodesk.com/community/gallery/project/25248/arduino-nano-v3)) and breadboard ([3dmakernow, 2019](https://www.autodesk.com/community/gallery/project/140202/arduino-breadboard-83x55mm)) were imported. The full assembly can be [viewed in 3D](https://a360.co/42HNbRR).

# Build Details

The goals for the penny were made by drilling large corner holes, cutting the openings with a jigsaw, and inserting custom 3D-printed parts so the penny could slide smoothly. Additional printed parts were used for the feet and for holding the electronic components in a compact form factor.

- **Laser engraved** plexiglass playing surface
- **Wooden frame** with dado cuts and miter joints
- **3D-printed** goal inserts, feet, and electronics holder
- **Assembly** via snap fitting, gluing, nailing, and screw-based methods

---

# Electronics

LEDs and electronic components were soldered and wired together using solid-core wire so the wiring could hold its shape. The project also pushed the Arduino code beyond simple single-action behavior, requiring control of LEDs, LCDs, a piezo, and pushbuttons in a way that felt simultaneous to the player.
